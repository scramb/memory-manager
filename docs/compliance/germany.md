# Germany: works council co-determination (template)

> **Not legal advice.** This is a template for an operator deploying `memory-manager` in Germany
> to consider works-council co-determination under the Betriebsverfassungsgesetz (BetrVG). It
> describes what this codebase actually logs, retains and makes visible - verified against the
> code, the ADRs and [`docs/security/threat-model.md`](../security/threat-model.md), not invented
> - so that an operator and their works council can assess it against §87(1) no. 6 BetrVG. It
> does not decide whether co-determination applies to your deployment, what a works agreement
> must contain, or how to run the consultation process. Have this reviewed by someone qualified to
> give legal advice for German labour law, and involve your works council, before relying on it.
> This section is **optional**: an operator outside Germany, or without a works council, has no
> need for it - the other three templates stand on their own.

## Why this might apply: §87(1) no. 6 BetrVG

§87(1) no. 6 BetrVG gives the works council (Betriebsrat) a co-determination right over the
introduction and use of **technical facilities designed to monitor the performance or behaviour
of employees** ("technische Einrichtungen, die dazu bestimmt sind, das Verhalten oder die
Leistung der Arbeitnehmer zu überwachen"). German case law reads "designed to" broadly: it is
enough that a system is **objectively suitable** (*geeignet*) for such monitoring, regardless of
whether the employer intends to use it that way. This is why the question is worth asking even
though `memory-manager`'s stated purpose is memory for Claude, not employee monitoring.

**What in this codebase is objectively suitable for monitoring, and what is not:**

| Feature | Suitable for performance/behaviour monitoring? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Note content (what an employee chose to write) | No, on its own | It is the employee's own voluntary input, not a record generated by observing their behaviour; see `toms.md` "Art. 25", "Collection of further personal data is opt-in" |
| Audit log (`audit_log`) - actor, client, operation, path, outcome, timestamp | **Yes, potentially** | It is a timestamped, per-employee record of every write action (`audit.py`'s own docstring, cited throughout [`toms.md`](./toms.md)); even though it exists for security monitoring and incident response (see [`data-flow.md`](./data-flow.md) "Purposes"), its content is objectively capable of showing *when* and *how often* an employee used the system |
| Observability traces/metrics (if `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` is set) | **Yes, potentially** | Per-request traces carry actor/client/operation attributes, never note content (`data-flow.md` "Observability") - but a request-rate or timing pattern per employee is still derivable from them |
| Write-rate/storage quotas (`QUOTA_*`, off by default) | **Yes, potentially, if enabled** | A per-user write-rate budget is, by construction, a per-employee activity counter (`toms.md` "Write-rate and storage quotas") |
| Break-glass access to a personal namespace | No, by itself - but its *existence* is a fact to co-determine | It is an exceptional, audited, notified path (`roles-and-permissions.md` "Break-glass"), not an ongoing monitoring mechanism; a works council's interest here is in the *process* (who can trigger it, under what control), not in treating it as surveillance |
| Deprovisioning via Graph delta sync | No | It only detects `accountEnabled`/removal, not behaviour (`deletion-concept.md` "Deprovisioning via Graph delta sync") |

The conclusion most operators reach is that **the audit log and (if enabled) observability
export and quotas** are the features to cover in a works agreement, not the memory feature
itself - but this is the operator's and works council's own determination, not this document's.

## What a works agreement (Betriebsvereinbarung) typically covers

A checklist an operator can take into a consultation, built from what this codebase actually
does and does not restrict on its own:

- [ ] **Purpose limitation.** State explicitly that `audit_log` (and, if enabled, the
  observability export and quota counters) are used **only** for security monitoring, incident
  response, and operating the service - never for individual performance evaluation. This
  codebase enforces the *mechanism* (metadata only, never note content, `audit.DETAIL_ALLOWLIST` -
  see `toms.md`) but the **purpose restriction itself is organisational**, not something the
  software can enforce; the agreement is where it becomes binding.
- [ ] **No performance evaluation.** Explicitly exclude using write counts, timing, or quota
  utilisation in any employee appraisal, probation decision or disciplinary process. Record this
  as a binding commitment, not an assumption - the data is objectively capable of being misused
  this way even though that is not its purpose.
- [ ] **Audit-log access.** Name who may read `audit_log` rows (typically a security/IT role, not
  line management) and under what circumstances - see [`roles-and-permissions.md`](./roles-and-permissions.md)
  "Operator access to the database" for the one access path this codebase cannot itself restrict
  (direct database access by whoever holds `DATABASE_URL`). The agreement should name that role
  by title, independent of which individual currently holds it.
- [ ] **Break-glass.** Co-determine the break-glass procedure described in
  [`roles-and-permissions.md`](./roles-and-permissions.md): who may be an approver
  (`Memory.Admin`), the default four-eyes requirement (`BREAK_GLASS_APPROVERS=2` - a works
  agreement is a reasonable place to require that this is **not** lowered to 1 without renewed
  consultation), the 1-hour expiry, and - most relevant to co-determination - that **every grant
  notifies the affected employee** (ADR-0008 addendum "Break-glass notification"), so break-glass
  is never covert.
- [ ] **Deletion and retention.** Reference [`deletion-concept.md`](./deletion-concept.md) for
  the concrete retention periods (`PERSONAL_RETENTION_DAYS`, default 30 days; the backup horizon,
  default 37 days) and record the organisation's own configured values in the agreement, so
  employees and the works council can verify them against the running deployment rather than
  trusting a number that could drift from the actual configuration.
- [ ] **Observability and quotas, if enabled.** If the operator turns on
  `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` or any `QUOTA_*` budget, name that explicitly in the agreement
  before enabling it - both are off by default (`toms.md` "Art. 25", "Collection of further
  personal data is opt-in"), so enabling either is a deployment decision the works council should
  see in advance, not discover afterwards.
- [ ] **Transparency to employees.** Reference [`transparency-notice.md`](./transparency-notice.md)
  as the document employees actually receive; the works agreement and the transparency notice
  should not contradict each other.
- [ ] `<any further item specific to your organisation's own deployment decisions - e.g. a
  non-default BLOCKLIST_FILE content policy, or a specific SIEM target and who has access to it>`.

## Not included

- A determination of whether §87(1) no. 6 BetrVG actually applies to your deployment - that
  depends on facts about your organisation (works council existence, how the tool is introduced)
  this document cannot know.
- A model Betriebsvereinbarung text - the checklist above is deliberately items to negotiate, not
  clauses to sign unchanged.
- Legal review of this template, or advice on German labour law generally.
